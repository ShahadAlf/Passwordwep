Password Tips 💡

Simple best practices to make your passwords stronger.

Best Practices
🧠
Do not reuse the same password for different accounts.
If one account is compromised, others are at risk.
🎲
Use a long passphrase instead of a short word.
Example: ILoveCoffee@Morning2025!
🔐
Enable Two-Factor Authentication (2FA).
Even if someone knows your password, they still need a second code.
⏰
Change important passwords regularly.
Especially email and banking accounts.
🚫
Never share your password with anyone.
Even if they say they are “support” or “IT”.
📱
Avoid saving passwords in plain notes or screenshots.
Use a trusted password manager instead.

Password Attacks ⚠️

Learn about common attack types and how to defend.

Security Awareness
💣 Brute Force Try all possibilities
⌄

Description: The attacker tries all possible passwords until one works.

Example: Trying every 6-digit PIN from 000000 to 999999.

Defense: Use long passwords and enable account lockout after several failed attempts.

Scenario: An attacker writes a script that sends thousands of different passwords to a login form every minute.

📚 Dictionary Attack Word list
⌄

Description: The attacker uses a list of common words and passwords.

Example: Trying: password, 123456, qwerty, welcome.

Defense: Avoid common words and predictable patterns.

Scenario: An attacker loads a file of 10,000 popular passwords and tests them automatically on a website login.

🎣 Phishing Tricking the user
⌄

Description: The attacker sends fake messages or websites that look real.

Example: A fake “bank” email with a link that asks you to “verify” your password.

Defense: Check the URL, do not click suspicious links, and use 2FA.

Scenario: A user receives an email that looks like it is from their university and asks them to log in on a fake portal.

👀 Shoulder Surfing Looking at the screen
⌄

Description: Someone watches you while you type your password.

Example: A person behind you in a lab or café.

Defense: Be aware of your environment and use a privacy screen if needed.

Scenario: In a busy computer lab, a student pretends to use their phone while secretly watching another student type their password.
